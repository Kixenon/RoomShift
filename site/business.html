The business case

A weekend problem. A weekend price.

People rearrange a room a few times a year: moving into a hall, setting up a desk, buying a fan before a Hong Kong summer. RoomShift is built for those weekends. It’s a furnished-room simulator that runs in a browser tab and is paid for once, not every month.

Who pays first

One customer to start with, and two who follow once the core works.

  1. Students and renters in small Hong Kong flats

    A few hundred square feet, a long humid summer, and a fan, an AC unit, a desk and a bed all competing for the same metres. The question is concrete (“where does the fan go?”), the need comes in bursts, and nobody wants another subscription for it.

    Room Pass, paid once

  2. Interior designers and space planners

    They compare layouts for a living, and a side-by-side render of the air and light in two proposals is something a client can understand.

    Studio subscription

  3. Small offices

    Where the heater goes and how desks are spaced shows up as comfort complaints. It comes later, once calibration earns the trust that reporting needs.

    Per-project licence, later

Where RoomShift sits

Two kinds of tools already exist, built for different people. Room planners make fitting furniture easy. Engineering simulators make the physics deep. A few consumer airflow apps sit closer to us, and they’re worth learning from.

Room planners and scanners
magicplan, Layoutr, Polycam. Excellent at getting a room and its furniture into the computer. Built for fit and looks, not for air, heat or light.
Engineering simulators
SimScale, DesignBuilder CFD. Real CFD with furniture and HVAC, priced and designed for professionals with time to set a model up.
Consumer airflow apps
CrossBreeze, Sim your flow. The closest to us: floor plans, fans and ventilation advice. Our bet is the furnished 3D room, three layers in one tab, and side-by-side comparison.

We don’t claim nobody else does this. Our claim is narrower: setting up a furnished room and comparing two arrangements of it should take minutes, not an engineering course.

Why now

1,500,000

cells at most, solved on your own GPU

WebGPU now ships in mainstream browsers, so a 3D airflow solve runs inside a tab. There’s nothing to install, and no cloud bill per simulation.

5 cm

grid, fine enough for furniture

Chair legs, desk edges and a sofa’s back all shape the flow at this resolution. That’s the difference between a furnished room and an empty box.

0 servers

doing the maths

The compute runs on the customer’s device, so a sale costs us almost nothing to serve. That’s what makes a one-time price workable.

Getting to the first thousand rooms

  1. Hall move-in

    Late August, when students move into university halls with a desk, a bed and a fan in a room they’ve never seen. Hall associations and student groups reach them in one week.

  2. Cooling season

    From May, people buy fans and portable AC units and wonder where they go. A “try it in your room first” link from electronics retailers and review sites costs them nothing.

  3. Designers as a channel

    Every Studio report a designer sends a client is a RoomShift render in front of a homeowner.

What could go wrong

And how we plan to find out early.

“Is it accurate?”
Our model check already shows where the jet diverges from theory. We label every result as an estimate, and calibration against measured fans comes before any accuracy claim.
One-time use
That’s the premise, not a bug. The Room Pass is priced for one weekend, and repeat use comes from moving, buying and designers.
Setup takes too long
Room size from a photo is already in development, and phone scanning comes next. The test we’ll run: can someone rebuild their furnished bedroom in under ten minutes?
Willingness to pay
Unknown. First test: a priced checkout page shown to renters who’ve just used the free editor.
