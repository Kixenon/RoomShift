RoomShift is a 3D editor for the room you already live in. Rebuild it in a few minutes, rearrange it freely, and see how each layout changes the air, warmth and light, before you drag anything across the floor.

Open the editor See two layouts compared

A layout can look right and still work badly.

  • The fan in the corner

    It takes up almost no floor space, and it cools mostly the wall behind it.

  • The heater behind the sofa

    It warms a pocket of air you never sit in, while the seat stays cool.

  • The lamp across the room

    The desk ends up lit from the wrong side, so your own shadow falls across the page.

Usually you find out after the furniture has moved. RoomShift lets you find out first.

How it works today

  1. Size the room

    Type the width, depth and height in metres. The walls follow.

  2. Place what’s in it

    Add a box, give it a model and a size, and drag it with the gizmo or type an exact position and rotation. Every move can be undone.

  3. Add windows

    Put them on any wall and open them to let air and heat out.

  4. Turn on a layer

    Air and Heat re-solve in the background as you edit. Light updates as you drag.

Where RoomShift is headed

The idea is bigger than an editor: tell RoomShift what matters to you, and it proposes arrangements and explains what each one gives up. Here’s what exists today, what’s being built, and what comes later.

  1. Available now

    • 3D room editor with seven furniture models, windows and undo
    • Airflow and heat estimates on the GPU, with a CPU fallback
    • Real-time light and shadow preview
    • 3D, top-down and orthographic views

  2. In development

    • Infrared view. Air temperature mapped onto the walls, floor and furniture.
    • Adjustable sources. Fan, heater and lamp strength, a fan on/off switch, and windows that act as an inlet or an outlet.
    • Room size from a photo. Click the four floor corners in a doorway photo, enter your camera height, and RoomShift estimates the width, depth and height.

  3. Planned

    • Priorities. Choose what matters in this room: daylight at the desk, air where you sit, walking space, warmth.
    • Layout generation. Alternatives that respect furniture sizes, doors and clearances, without dragging every piece yourself.
    • Scores you can read. Each layout scored against your priorities, with the trade-offs spelled out instead of one “perfect” answer.

What the colours mean, and what they don’t

RoomShift’s layers are estimates for comparing one layout with another. They aren’t measurements, and they aren’t certified engineering.

  • Airflow and heat come from a simplified 3D fluid model. It isn’t validated CFD, and it leaves out turbulence models, wall heat capacity and HVAC.
  • Heater strength is an estimated warming rate, not watts, and the temperatures shown are estimates, not readings.
  • Light is a shadow preview, not calibrated lux. It doesn’t model light bouncing between surfaces.

So use it to ask “is B better than A?”, not “is this room safe?”.
